What the app is, and what it is not

GyroHop estimates where your telescope points from the phone's motion sensors and the stars you align on, and shows which way to push the tube. The phone does not see the sky. How close it gets depends on how firmly the phone sits on the tube, how carefully you aligned, how long ago, and the sensors in your particular phone, which drift over time. The app shows its expected error on the screen; treat it as a guide to the right patch of sky, and find the object in the eyepiece yourself.

Rise times and the times an object can next be seen are worked out for the position you gave and a clear, flat horizon. Weather, trees, buildings, light pollution and the Moon decide what you actually see.

The app is not for navigation, surveying or anything where a wrong direction could harm someone.

The Sun

Never point a telescope, a finder or binoculars at or near the Sun without a proper solar filter fitted over the front of the instrument. Looking at the Sun through them, even for a moment, can blind you. The app never guides you to the Sun, but a tube swung across the daytime sky can pass it. Use the app after sunset, or with a filter fitted, and keep the finder covered.
