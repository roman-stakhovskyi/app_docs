Privacy Policy

GyroHop · last updated 7 November 2026

The short version. GyroHop works on your phone, without an internet connection. Your position on Earth sets where the stars are in your sky; it is kept on your phone and never sent to us. There is no account to make. Some things leave the phone without you doing anything: a crash or error report, and a check whether this version still works. Others leave only when you do something: a message or feature request you send us, and opening or voting on the feature board. We keep messages so we can answer them, delete them whenever you ask, and may delete them sooner ourselves.

What the app keeps on your phone

Your position, your settings (how the phone sits on the tube, the mount, the color of the screen and the rest), the stars you aligned on with the phone's orientation at each one, the objects you looked at last, and the explanations you have already dismissed are written to storage that belongs to the app on your own device. So is a random install identifier the app creates for itself, and any crash report waiting to be sent. We cannot read any of it. Deleting the app removes it from that phone; a copy in a backup of the phone stays until you delete it there.

Your location

The app needs to know roughly where you are, because the sky looks different from every place. You can type your latitude and longitude, or tap Use GPS. Only then does the app ask for permission to use your location, and only while you are using it. It asks for your approximate location, not your precise one: a few kilometers make no difference to where the stars are. It takes one reading, saves the latitude and longitude on your phone and stops; it does not follow you.

Your location is used on your phone to work out where each object is in your sky and when it can next be seen. It is never sent to us, never put in a crash report, a message or a feature request, and never shared with anyone.

Motion sensors

To tell which way your telescope points, the app reads the phone's orientation from its gyroscope and accelerometer while the guidance screen is open. It does not use the compass. These readings are not sent anywhere. The only ones kept are the readings taken when you align on a star or mark an object as seen, stored on your phone as part of your alignment.

Backups of your phone

  • Android: Android's own Auto Backup may copy the app's data, your position and settings included, as part of the system backup of your phone. That is a Google feature, configured in your device settings.
  • iPhone: your device's own iCloud or computer backup includes the app's data, under your Apple Account.

When you write to us, or use the feature board

If you send a message through Contact Support or post a feature request, we receive what you wrote, the email address you give us if you give one, the app's version, whether you use iOS or Android, and the random install identifier, which lets us tie a reply to the right conversation. That identifier is not your name, your phone number or your advertising id, and it is created by the app itself.

A feature request is public inside the app. Its title and description appear on the board for everyone who uses GyroHop, so do not put anything personal in them. Your email address never appears there.

Opening the board and voting send the same install identifier, which is stored with your vote.

A support conversation is kept so we can answer it and so the answer is still there if you write again; a feature request is kept because the board is the list of what people have asked for. We do not use any of it for anything else. You are not required to write to us or to use the board, and nothing in the app depends on it.

Crash and error reports

Released versions send a report automatically when the app crashes or an operation fails unexpectedly. A report carries technical details of the error, the time, the app version, the platform and OS version, the device model, and the same random install identifier. A report is about the code, not about you: it carries no location, no name and no email, though the install identifier is the same one a message you send us carries. A report that cannot be sent waits on the phone and goes the next time the app has a connection.

The check at launch

While a released version is open and has an internet connection, it listens to our service for whether this version is still supported or the service is down for maintenance, so we can ask you to update a version that no longer works. The request carries the app's identity and, as any internet request does, your network (IP) address; nothing you have entered, and not your location. Without a connection the app works as usual.

Network addresses

Like any internet service, ours sees the network (IP) address each request comes from; we use it only to stop abuse and keep it no longer than that needs.

Purchases

If you buy Pro, yearly or for good, the payment is handled entirely by Apple or Google. We never see your card, your billing address or your account details, only whether this install owns the purchase, so the app can give you what you paid for. That answer is kept on your phone.

Why we hold it

In the language of the GDPR, each use above rests on one of these grounds:

  • Performing our agreement with you: using your location and the sensors on your phone to point your telescope, which is what the app is for, and giving you Pro once you have bought it.
  • Our legitimate interest in answering you: support messages, feature requests and votes, so we can answer the person who wrote, know what people have asked for, and show what was said if a dispute about the app ever arises.
  • Our legitimate interest in an app that works: crash and error reports, which tell us what is broken, and the check at launch, which lets us stop a version that no longer works.
  • Our legitimate interest in a service that stays up: using network addresses to stop abuse.

Where we rely on a legitimate interest, you may object, and we will stop unless our reason outweighs yours. We make no automated decisions about you.

What we do not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics or usage tracking.
  • No selling of anything to anyone, and no sharing for anyone else's purposes.
  • No location leaves your phone, and the app never tracks where you go.
  • No contacts, camera, photos, microphone or health data.

Who else sees it

  • Our support and crash-reporting service: support messages, feature requests, votes, crash and error reports, the check at launch and network addresses go there, and nowhere else of ours. It runs on a cloud hosting provider in the United States, which processes the data for us and for no purpose of its own. Transfers outside the European Economic Area rely on the European Commission's adequacy decision for the United States (the EU-US Data Privacy Framework) or on the Commission's standard contractual clauses.
  • Apple: the App Store sale, the location permission on an iPhone, and your device backup. Apple holds those under your Apple Account, not ours (Apple's privacy policy).
  • Google: the Google Play sale, the location permission on Android, and Android's Auto Backup. Google holds those under your Google Account (Google's privacy policy). On some Android phones, Google's location services help the phone work out where it is when the app asks; that happens between your phone and Google, under your device settings.
  • Other people using the app: the title and description of a feature request you post, and nothing else.
  • The web host of these pages: it logs the network address of whoever opens them, for security. The pages set no cookies and load nothing from anyone else; even the typeface is served from the same place.

How long things are kept

  • On your phone: until you delete it, or delete the app. Typing a new position or using GPS again replaces the old one.
  • Backups of your phone: for as long as Apple or Google keep them under your account.
  • Messages and feature requests: until you ask us to remove them (see below) or until we remove them ourselves, which we may do at any time. We do not delete them on a timer, because a conversation you come back to is more use to you whole.
  • Votes: for as long as the feature board exists.
  • Crash and error reports: 90 days, then each report is deleted automatically.
  • Network addresses: only as long as stopping abuse needs.

Your choices

  • Location: type your position by hand instead of using GPS, and the app never asks for location permission. If you granted it, you can take it back at any time in your phone's settings; the position already saved stays until you change it.
  • Delete everything on the phone: remove the app. That does not remove a copy in a backup of your phone; delete it there.

Deleting a message or a feature request

Write to the address on this page and say what you want removed. Ask from the email address you used when you wrote to us: that address is how we find your messages, and we have no other way to tell which are yours.

We delete them from our service, along with anything else attached to that address, within 30 days, and reply when it is done.

If you wrote without giving an email address, there is no address to match, and nothing in what we hold points back to you. That also means we cannot find it to delete it. The same is true of votes and crash reports: they carry only the install identifier, which you cannot see, so we cannot pick out yours.

Your rights

Wherever you live, and in the EU and the UK as a matter of law, you may ask us to show you what we hold about you, correct it, delete it, restrict what we do with it, or hand it to you in a form you can take elsewhere; and you may object to us holding it at all. Ask at the address on this page.

If you think we have handled your data badly and our answer does not satisfy you, you may complain to the data protection authority of the country you live in. We would rather you wrote to us first, but that right is yours either way.

Children

The app is made for people who observe with a telescope and is not directed at children under 13. It asks for nothing about who you are. A child who uses it with an adult sends us nothing unless they write to us.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the updated page replaces this one. If a change affects what leaves your phone, the store notes of the update that brings it say so, so you know before you install it.
